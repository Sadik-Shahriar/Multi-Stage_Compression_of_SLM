\begin{abstract}
The energy cost of language-model inference is a growing Green Computing concern. This paper reports an implementation-aware ablation of AWQ weight quantization, custom PyramidKV-style token eviction, and TurboQuant numerical KV quantization on Llama 3.2-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct using an NVIDIA T4. Each model was measured at 1024, 2048, and 4096 tokens with five energy blocks, 10~Hz NVML monitoring, and a fixed 64-token decode; a frozen hard-retrieval benchmark used 60 tokenizer-specific 4096-token scenarios per configuration. At 4096 tokens, Llama eviction used $529.30\pm20.02$~J and scored 37/60 versus 38/60 for its matched-wrapper AWQ control. Qwen eviction used $550.48\pm10.07$~J but scored 1/60 versus 48/60 for AWQ, while Qwen TQ-Ref preserved 49/60 retrieval at $812.28\pm13.11$~J. Raw, 82~W-clipped, and 70~W-clipped trace integrations preserved registered rankings and significance decisions. The accessible TurboQuant 0.2.0 path was numerically active but stored uint8 indices, materialized K/V, and used unfused execution. A CPU-only realization-gap analysis proved lower persistent-KV storage for declared ideal packed layouts, but retained energy as the conditional relation $E_{ideal}=E_{baseline}-\epsilon_{mem}\Delta_D+O_{net}$ rather than fabricating projected joules. The results show that energy savings do not imply quality preservation and that nominal precision must be separated from physical realization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================

Training and serving large neural models require substantial computational resources \cite{strubell_energy_2019,patterson_carbon_nodate}. As model use scales, inference efficiency becomes a recurring systems concern rather than a one-time training cost.

A central inference bottleneck is the Key-Value (KV) cache inside decoder-only transformers. During generation, the model stores key and value tensors for previous tokens to avoid recomputation. This state grows linearly with sequence length and increases memory traffic during every decode step \cite{liu_kivi_2023,zhang_rdkv_2026}.

This study operationally uses ``SLM'' for models below 7 billion parameters. Such models can reduce resource demand and run on lower-cost hardware \cite{husom_sustainable_2025}, but their energy advantage remains workload- and hardware-dependent. They still have the KV-cache problem, especially for long inputs.

The research community has developed three main techniques to address this problem: weight quantization, token eviction, and KV-cache quantization. These techniques are usually studied separately. We found no prior study in the reviewed literature that evaluated their three-way composition on a sub-4B SLM while tracking real GPU power draw.

This study addresses that gap through reproducible Llama 3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B case studies on a single NVIDIA T4. Their different GQA geometries provide a bounded portability test without treating architecture alone as a causal explanation.

The problem is explicitly multi-objective. A configuration that minimizes energy may not minimize latency or memory, and the most compressed configuration may not preserve retrieval quality. The study therefore evaluates Pareto dominance across energy, latency, memory, throughput, and quality rather than assuming that maximum compression is automatically best.

\section{Background}
% ============================================================

To understand this study, it helps to know about transformer KV caches, the three compression strategies, and the selected Llama model.

\textbf{KV Cache Growth}: Transformers store key and value tensors for previous tokens to avoid recalculating them. Cache storage grows as $O(n \times L \times h_{kv} \times d)$, where $n$ is context length, $L$ is the number of layers, $h_{kv}$ is the number of KV heads, and $d$ is head dimension. Its repeated movement between GPU memory levels can make long-context decode memory-bound \cite{li_snapkv_nodate,liu_kivi_2023}.

\textbf{Weight Quantization}: AWQ \cite{lin_awq_2025} protects activation-salient weights while quantizing most model weights to low precision. Its published system combines the algorithm with optimized kernels; therefore, reported speedups are hardware- and implementation-dependent and are not assumed in advance for the T4.

\textbf{Token Eviction}: This approach removes tokens from the KV cache that are not getting much attention. H2O \cite{zhang_h2o_nodate} showed that just 20\% of tokens receive most of the attention. SnapKV \cite{li_snapkv_nodate} (NeurIPS 2024) improved on this by using the last 32 tokens as a ``window'' to predict which older tokens will matter, reporting a 3.6$\times$ generation speedup and 8.2$\times$ memory reduction at 16K tokens. PyramidKV \cite{cai_pyramidkv_2025} (COLM 2025) goes further: it noticed that early layers in a transformer spread attention broadly, while later layers focus on just a few tokens. By giving more cache space to early layers and less to later ones, it achieves 5--15\% better retrieval accuracy than SnapKV at the same memory budget.

\textbf{KV Cache Quantization}: TurboQuant \cite{zandieh_turboquant_2025} applies a data-oblivious random rotation followed by an optimized scalar codebook. The paper reports quality-neutral KV-cache results near 3.5 bits per channel, while related evidence favors preserving dimensions through quantization over rank reduction, particularly for GQA models \cite{salfati_quantization_2026}. This project uses the independent TurboQuant PyPI reference implementation, so algorithmic bit width, physical storage, and kernel efficiency are reported separately.

\textbf{Llama 3.2-3B}: The selected decoder model belongs to the Llama 3 family \cite{grattafiori_llama3_2024}. Its official model card and checkpoint configuration specify 28 transformer layers, 24 query heads, 8 KV heads, a head dimension of 128, and an original 8192-token rotary-position window \cite{meta_llama32_2024}. The experiment uses an FP16 checkpoint and a community AWQ checkpoint configured for 4-bit weights with group size 128 \cite{casperhansen_llama32_awq}. The tested contexts are limited to 4096 because the 8192 condition exceeded the available free-tier T4 memory during pilot development.

\section{Problem Statement}
% ============================================================

Existing work establishes the value of weight quantization, token eviction, and KV-cache quantization, while RDKV and ThinKV show that eviction and quantization should be considered jointly \cite{zhang_rdkv_2026,ramachandran_thinkv_2026}. However, there is limited empirical evidence on how all three compression axes interact on a sub-4B SLM when real GPU energy is measured. Published algorithmic compression ratios also do not guarantee lower wall-clock energy or runtime memory when kernels are unfused or higher-precision working tensors remain reachable.

The research problem is therefore the absence of an implementation-aware, multi-objective comparison of these techniques and their observed composition across context lengths. The study also addresses two practical uncertainties identified by the literature: how TurboQuant precision changes distortion and runtime cost, and how pyramidal retention budget changes the energy--quality trade-off.

\subsection{Aim}

\subsection{Objectives}

The executed Llama case study had the following objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Implement and validate} C0 (FP16), C1/C1-W (AWQ controls), C2 (AWQ + PyramidKV-style eviction), C3-Ref (AWQ + TQ-Ref), and C4-Ref (all three), with structural gates proving that the requested cache operations execute.
  \item \textbf{Measure energy efficiency} through prefill, decode, and total joules; idle-adjusted energy; and decode joules per token at 1024, 2048, and 4096 tokens.
  \item \textbf{Measure runtime performance} through prefill latency, decode latency, and throughput under an identical 64-token teacher-forced decode workload.
  \item \textbf{Measure memory efficiency} through peak VRAM, post-prefill memory, post-decode memory, actual cache bytes, and logical compression ratio.
  \item \textbf{Evaluate quality} using randomized NIAH prompts, reporting retrieval correctness separately from exact-format compliance.
  \item \textbf{Analyze compression sensitivity} using TurboQuant bit widths of 2, 3, and 4 and pyramidal average budgets of 512, 1024, and 1536 at 4096 tokens.
  \item \textbf{Quantify exploratory configuration non-additivity} for C4-Ref without treating the historical Llama contrast as a clean factorial estimator, because stock C1 and the wrapped paths differ.
  \item \textbf{Test differences} with paired procedures, confidence intervals, effect sizes, and multiple-comparison correction.
  \item \textbf{Identify Pareto-optimal choices} for energy-, latency-, memory-, and quality-constrained deployment scenarios.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Focus}

This study keeps hardware fixed to one T4 but replicates the five-path ablation on Llama 3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B. Effects are computed within model first; absolute joules, coincident block numbers, and tokenizer-specific prompts are not treated as cross-model paired observations.

We measure several things at once: energy, latency, memory, throughput, and retrieval quality. No single weighted score decides what is best. Instead, a configuration wins on Pareto grounds: it is better if it improves one metric without hurting another. Different deployment scenarios will favor different trade-offs.

\section{Contributions}
% ============================================================

This research makes three focused contributions. \textbf{First}, it provides cross-model hardware-energy evidence for AWQ, custom pyramidal eviction, and numerical TQ-Ref on two sub-4B SLMs. \textbf{Second}, it combines hard retrieval, matched-wrapper controls, trace-cap sensitivity, and uncertainty-aware within-model contrasts rather than assuming compression gains transfer across models. \textbf{Third}, it separates measured systems behavior, realized TQ-Ref storage, and ideal packed/fused counterfactuals through calibrated persistent-memory accounting, phase-specific logical byte demand, and symbolic energy break-even regions.

\section{Related Works}
% ============================================================

Researchers have studied various ways to compress language models. This section reviews the most relevant work across weight quantization, token eviction, KV cache quantization, compound methods, and energy measurement.

\textbf{Weight Quantization:} Lin et al.\ \cite{lin_awq_2025} developed AWQ, which protects activation-salient weights and enables low-bit deployment when paired with suitable kernels. Frantar et al.\ \cite{frantar_gptq_2023} proposed GPTQ as a second-order alternative. The broader SLMQuant benchmark \cite{wang_slmquant_2025} shows that compression behavior does not transfer uniformly from large models to SLMs, motivating direct measurement on Llama 3.2-3B.

\textbf{Token Eviction:} Zhang et al.\ \cite{zhang_h2o_nodate} showed that only about 20\% of tokens in the KV cache actually matter, which they called the Heavy Hitter Oracle. Li et al.\ \cite{li_snapkv_nodate} built on this with SnapKV, which uses a short observation window to figure out which tokens can be safely removed, achieving large memory savings at 16K context. Cai et al.\ \cite{cai_pyramidkv_2025} improved further with PyramidKV, which uses a pyramidal budget across layers and beats SnapKV by 5--15\% NIAH accuracy.

\textbf{KV Cache Quantization:} Zandieh et al.\ \cite{zandieh_turboquant_2025} proposed TurboQuant, which uses random rotation and an optimized scalar codebook without calibration data. Liu et al.\ \cite{liu_kivi_2023} offered KIVI as a 2-bit alternative. Salfati \cite{salfati_quantization_2026} showed that preserving dimensions through quantization can outperform rank reduction, with a growing advantage for GQA. These results motivate both TurboQuant and the executed bit-width sensitivity analysis.

\textbf{Compound Methods:} Zhang et al.\ \cite{zhang_rdkv_2026} jointly allocate eviction and quantization through a rate-distortion formulation and report strong long-context accuracy and throughput. ThinKV \cite{ramachandran_thinkv_2026} likewise uses hybrid quantization and eviction for reasoning models. These methods demonstrate that joint compression is important, but neither supplies the sub-4B T4 energy ablation of AWQ, custom PyramidKV-style eviction, and TQ-Ref reported here. The present C4-Ref stack remains sequential rather than a reproduction of either joint optimizer.

\textbf{Energy Measurement:} Argerich et al.\ \cite{argerich_watt_2026} published Watt Counts (2026), a large benchmark covering 5,000+ LLM inference experiments. They established that T4 GPUs draw about 12W idle and up to 82W at peak, and selected 10 Hz (100 ms) polling via pynvml as the best accuracy--overhead trade-off after evaluating rates from 1 Hz to 200 Hz. Table~\ref{tab:comparison} summarizes selected related work.

\section{Research Questions}
% ============================================================

The main research question is: when AWQ, PyramidKV-style eviction, and numerical TQ-Ref are applied individually and together on Llama 3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B, how do their energy, latency, memory, throughput, and hard-retrieval trade-offs change on a T4 GPU, and which findings transfer across models?

This breaks down into four specific sub-questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RQ1:} How do the five core configurations affect prefill and decode energy, latency, throughput, peak and persistent memory, and NIAH retrieval at 1024, 2048, and 4096 tokens?

\item \textbf{RQ2:} How do TQ-Ref bit width and pyramidal average retention budget change numerical distortion and the energy--quality--memory trade-off, and what interaction appears in C4-Ref?

\item \textbf{RQ3:} Which configurations are Pareto-optimal for energy-, latency-, memory-, and quality-constrained deployment scenarios?
\item \textbf{RQ4:} How do within-model effects, hard retrieval, and realization gaps differ between Llama and Qwen without treating their separate sessions or tokenizer-specific prompts as paired observations?
\end{enumerate}

Based on the literature and implementation-aware pilot observations, the historical Llama systems matrix evaluated the following directional hypotheses; the Qwen replication tests portability rather than retroactively changing them:

\textbf{H1 (AWQ context effect):} The energy advantage of AWQ over FP16 increases with context length.

\textbf{H2 (pyramidal-eviction long-context benefit):} At 4096 tokens, C2 reduces decode energy per token and increases throughput relative to C1 while keeping retrieval accuracy within five percentage points.

\textbf{H3 (reference-configuration overhead):} The unfused TurboQuant 0.2.0 configuration C3-Ref increases decode latency and energy relative to C1 at the tested contexts; wrapper and kernel profiling are required to isolate the mechanism.

\textbf{H4 (compound configuration pattern):} At 4096 tokens, C4-Ref uses less energy and lower latency than C3-Ref but more energy and lower throughput than C2, producing a non-zero configuration interaction contrast.

\textbf{H5 (Context-dependent ranking):} The relative efficiency ranking changes with context length, with eviction-based configurations becoming more beneficial as context grows.

For each directional hypothesis, the corresponding null states that the specified contrast or interaction is absent after multiple-comparison correction.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
% ============================================================

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{methodology_diagram.png}
\caption{Methodological framework for the two-model empirical ablation, hard-retrieval evaluation, trace robustness, and CPU-only realization-gap synthesis.}
\label{fig:methodology}
\end{figure}

Both model experiments ran on a single NVIDIA T4 in Google Colab with PyTorch 2.11.0, Transformers 5.12.1, GPTQModel 7.1.0, and TurboQuant 0.2.0, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:methodology}. Model revisions and the TurboQuant package-source hash were recorded. Llama used C0, stock C1, C2, C3-Ref, and C4-Ref for its historical context matrix; Qwen used Q0, matched-wrapper Q1-W, Q2, Q3-Ref, and Q4-Ref. Both systems matrices covered 1024, 2048, and 4096 tokens with five measured blocks per condition. Llama also added a separate-session five-block C1-W control at 4096. Qwen Q0 occupied a separate model-resident phase, while its AWQ paths shared the validated Qwen wrapper.

Each systems run produced 75 core and 20 sensitivity energy rows with 95 raw traces. Historical Llama quality contained 225 easy core and 60 sensitivity rows, while the accepted hard follow-up produced 300 rows across C0, C1-W, C2, C3-Ref, and C4-Ref. Qwen produced 300 hard core and 60 sensitivity-quality rows. The hard endpoint uses 60 deterministic semantic scenarios at 10\%, 50\%, and 90\% depth; Llama and Qwen tokenize and pad them independently to exactly 4096 tokens.

\textbf{Environment and energy:} The FP16 baseline used \texttt{unsloth/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct}; AWQ used \texttt{casperhansen/llama-3.2-3b-instruct-awq}. GPU power was sampled at 10~Hz via pynvml \cite{argerich_watt_2026} and trapezoidally integrated over measured timestamps. Raw traces, temperature, clocks, throttling flags, power limits, allocated VRAM, and NVML memory were persisted. The model-resident median idle powers were 29.116~W for FP16 and 30.183~W for AWQ.

\textbf{Metrics:} Energy used a fixed 64-token teacher-forced decode so that measured work was identical across configurations. Historical easy quality used five seeds at each of three depths; the primary hard endpoint used 20 independent scenarios per depth, or 60 per configuration. Retrieval correctness and exact-format compliance were scored separately. Memory was reported as peak allocated VRAM, post-prefill and post-decode memory, logical retained FP16 bytes, and deduplicated live cache storage. Sequential post-compression perplexity was outside the computational scope; a prefill-only loss would not capture the modified decode cache.

\textbf{Implementation and validation:} C2, C3-Ref, and C4-Ref used a custom Llama attention wrapper; Q1-W through Q4-Ref used a separately validated Qwen adapter that passed stock-versus-wrapper parity before measurement. The PyramidKV-style path used a 32-token observation window, kernel-5 average pooling, per-KV-head top-$k$ selection, and a linear $\beta=20$ layer budget; it was not the official KVCache-Factory implementation. Every path checked layer count, retained lengths, requested budget, TQ-Ref bit width, index dtype and range, residual length, active decode use, and alias-deduplicated storage. TurboQuant 0.2.0 stored one uint8 index per coordinate at every tested bit width and retained materialized K/V; its nominal bit setting is numerical precision, not packed physical storage.

\textbf{Analysis:} Registered within-session comparisons used paired $t$-tests and Wilcoxon checks on five common blocks with 95\% confidence intervals, effect sizes, and Holm correction. The historical Llama difference-in-differences contrast remains wrapper-confounded; Qwen uses Q1-W as its matched baseline. Hard quality uses Wilson intervals and paired depth-stratified bootstrap contrasts. Cross-model energy and quality are resampled independently because neither model nor major sessions share a sampling unit.

\textbf{Cross-model and counterfactual synthesis:} The Qwen replication used Q0, Q1-W, Q2, Q3-Ref, and Q4-Ref under the same semantic hard scenarios but independent tokenizer materialization. A local CPU reference execution of Notebook~09 verified all source manifests, canonical shards/CSV correspondence, and 195 raw traces before analysis; the final reproducibility package reserves authority for the pending Colab CPU rerun. Cross-model effects used independent resampling; pairing was retained only inside registered sessions and within-depth scenario IDs. The ideal packed layout was calibrated against retained FP bytes and uint8 TQ payload/category bytes. Prefill and decode logical demand were modeled separately. Projected energy was not point-identified: break-even requires $O_{net}<\epsilon_{mem}\Delta_D$, with nonnegative energy and explicit quality-equivalence conditions.

\subsection{Cache Storage and TurboQuant Realization}

C2 reduced the 4096-token live cache from 455~MiB to 119~MiB, a 73.8\% reduction relative to C1. C4-Ref reduced the cache to 187.25~MiB, but remained 57.4\% larger than C2 because TQ-Ref added index, norm, residual, and materialized-view storage.

The TQ-Ref bit width did not represent physical packed width in version 0.2.0. For 2-, 3-, and 4-bit settings, the implementation stored one uint8 index per coordinate and retained materialized K/V. Consequently, all three bit-width conditions had the same physical cache size. C3-Ref's 696.5~MiB live cache was larger than the unquantized 455~MiB cache. The valid interpretation is therefore a numerical codebook and runtime-overhead study, not a demonstration of packed 2--4-bit cache storage.

\subsection{Compound Interaction}

C4-Ref consumed $659.50\pm21.35$~J, between C2 and C3-Ref. It used 253.98~J less energy than C3-Ref ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.000206$), but 130.21~J more than C2 ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.00620$). The reported configuration contrast was
\begin{equation}
I=(C4\text{-Ref}-C3\text{-Ref})-(C2-C1),
\end{equation}
Total-energy contrast was $-152.03$~J (95\% CI: $-187.11$ to $-116.96$~J; Holm-adjusted $p=0.00109$). Decode-energy and decode-time contrasts were also significant. The pattern supports H4 and is consistent with an ``energy shield'': C4-Ref applies TQ-Ref after retaining fewer tokens. Because C1 did not use the custom wrapper, the contrast is not an uncontaminated factorial estimate and cannot attribute the entire value solely to cache-operation interaction. C4-Ref was not preferable to eviction alone.

\subsection{Historical Easy Retrieval and Format Quality}

Every one of the 225 historical easy-NIAH cases retrieved the requested code. Exact-format compliance was lower: 188/225 responses (83.6\%) returned only the requested case-sensitive code. These rows are retained as historical behavior evidence, not the primary quality endpoint.

The 100\% rate establishes only that the historical prompt set had a ceiling. It is superseded for quality claims by the frozen hard60 endpoint reported below. In C4-Ref, the fixed 64-token energy decode eventually quantized all 28 layers, whereas shorter quality generations could leave the smallest-budget layer inside TQ-Ref's 128-token residual window; quality and energy therefore observed slightly different realized cache states.

\subsection{Sensitivity Findings}

Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity} reports the two focused studies. Pyramidal budget had only a small descriptive energy effect: 512, 1024, and 1536 average tokens produced $525.65\pm10.82$, $529.30\pm20.02$, and $536.49\pm17.13$~J, respectively, with 100\% retrieval in each condition. The differences are small relative to their standard deviations and were not subjected to a planned inferential contrast; no strong budget optimum should be claimed from these data.

TurboQuant distortion changed monotonically with precision. Averaged over sampled key and value vectors, normalized MSE fell from 0.1262 at 2 bits to 0.0475 at 3 bits and 0.0190 at 4 bits. Energy increased from $890.11\pm4.00$~J at 2 bits to $895.38\pm2.26$~J at 3 bits and $913.48\pm2.11$~J at 4 bits. The 2-bit path retrieved 14/15 cases (93.3\%); 3- and 4-bit paths retrieved 15/15. A single miss is only a suggestive quality signal, while the numerical distortion trend is clear. No physical memory trade-off was observed.

\subsection{Pareto Uncertainty and Decision Boundaries}

Pareto claims are interpreted as finite-sample decision aids rather than proofs of population optimality. For Llama, the original easy benchmark had a retrieval ceiling, so its uncertainty-aware frontier is not used for quality conclusions. The hard60 endpoint instead places C0, C1-W, and C2 on the measured energy--retrieval point frontier: C2 offers the lowest observed energy among them, while C0 retains the highest observed retrieval. Their Wilson intervals overlap substantially, and C2's paired difference from C1-W is only $-1/60$ (depth-stratified bootstrap 95\% interval $[-5,0]$ percentage points). Thus, the data do not establish a strict five-point non-inferiority claim for eviction on Llama.

For Qwen, the accepted bootstrap Pareto analysis gives empirical membership frequencies of 0.984 for Q0, 0.3575 for Q1-W, 1.000 for Q2, 0.030 for Q3-Ref, and 0 for Q4-Ref. These are resampling selection frequencies, not probabilities that a configuration is truly optimal. Q2 is selected because it has the lowest observed energy and storage, but its 1/60 hard-retrieval result makes it unsuitable for quality-constrained deployment. The uncertainty analysis therefore reinforces, rather than overrides, the energy--quality trade-off: a Pareto label cannot substitute for a stated minimum-quality requirement.

\subsection{Ideal-TQ Realization Gap}

The aligned packed-memory model reproduced accepted retained-FP bytes and uint8 logical payloads before projecting nominal 2/3/4-bit storage. At four bits, projected post-decode persistent KV was 131.14~MiB for Llama TQ-only and 42.15~MiB for Qwen TQ-only, versus 455~MiB and 146.25~MiB retained FP state, respectively. Compound ideal footprints were 41.89~MiB for Llama and 13.46~MiB for Qwen. These are persistent-KV results under a perfectly packed layout, not peak-VRAM measurements.

The phase model counted full-prompt prefill reads, retained-state packing reads/writes, growing decode reads, residual traffic, and residual-to-packed transitions. It models compulsory/logical demand, not exact DRAM traffic. Because no justified finite domains were available for $\epsilon_{mem}$ and $O_{net}$, ideal joules and fused latency were not numerically projected. Point, conservative, and possible quality frontiers were therefore reported as symbolic all/some/none regions over quality threshold, realization overhead, and bounded implementation-quality change.

% ============================================================
\section{Discussion}
% ============================================================

The dominant practical result is that the eviction configuration, not numerical KV quantization, provided the clearest core T4 trade-off in this implementation. C2 was associated with lower energy, higher throughput, and lower cache storage simultaneously at 4096 tokens. This pattern is consistent with reduced decode memory traffic, but no hardware memory-traffic counters were collected. AWQ also showed a context-dependent crossover, but kernel-level profiling would be required to establish its mechanism.

The TQ-Ref result illustrates why nominal algorithmic precision must be separated from realized systems behavior. The reference package preserved the paper's rotation and codebook idea, and its measured distortion improved with bit width, but its live object graph retained materialized higher-precision tensors. In the tested implementation, quantization was associated with additional work and storage rather than a realized systems saving. C4-Ref's lower cost than C3-Ref is consistent with applying that path after eviction, but wrapper and kernel profiling are needed to establish the causal mechanism.

This sequential interaction is not equivalent to RDKV's rate--distortion allocation or ThinKV's thought-adaptive joint policy. A direct joint-method baseline was not available under the present Llama/T4 software constraints. The appropriate comparison is conceptual and empirical: the results show why joint allocation can matter, but do not claim to outperform those systems.


% ============================================================
\section{Threats to Validity and Ongoing Work}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Retrieval scope:} The historical easy NIAH set had a ceiling, so the primary endpoint is now the frozen hard 60-scenario set with Wilson and depth-stratified bootstrap uncertainty. Each model has tokenizer-specific exact 4096-token prompts; semantic matching does not make tokenized inputs identical.

\textbf{Power telemetry:} Although all traces integrated consistently, the artifact audit found instantaneous NVML samples above the reported 70~W enforced limit, including isolated values above 100~W. Such excursions may reflect the difference between instantaneous telemetry and an averaged power cap, but they are suspicious for a T4. A post-hoc 70~W clipping audit changed core group means by approximately 0.6--2.2\% and did not alter rankings; including sensitivity groups raised the maximum change to approximately 2.4\%. Absolute joules should nevertheless be reported with this caveat, and raw-versus-capped robustness should accompany the final analysis.

\textbf{Statistical power, wrapper, and ordering:} Five blocks provide limited distributional evidence. The large C1--C2, C1--C3-Ref, C2--C4-Ref, and C3-Ref--C4-Ref configuration differences remained clear after Holm correction, while C1--C4-Ref did not. C1 used stock attention while C2/C3-Ref/C4-Ref used the custom wrapper, so comparisons to C1 include wrapper effects and the interaction contrast is not a clean factorial estimator. C0 was measured in a separate phase, making FP16 comparisons and context-crossover claims descriptive. Sensitivity differences were also descriptive.

\textbf{External validity:} The evidence covers two 3B models, one T4, batch size one, contexts up to 4096, model-specific adapters, custom PyramidKV-style eviction, and one unfused TQ-Ref implementation. One major session per experiment and unmatched Llama/Qwen baseline structure prevent architecture-only causal attribution. The fixed teacher-forced decode is not an end-to-end natural generation workload.

\textbf{Reproducibility and realization:} Critical versions, manifests, raw traces, source hashes, and final inventories were recorded. Notebook~08 required two in-session repair cells that are preserved inline; its persisted evidence is hash-consistent, but neither its experiment fingerprint nor artifact manifest cryptographically binds the executed notebook or repair-cell source. The local provisional Notebook~09 run discloses that direct live tensor dumps were not preserved; post-hoc shape/dtype evidence uses runtime-asserted contracts plus persisted aggregate/category bytes. A clean Drive-mounted Colab version is reserved for the authoritative synthesis rerun. Ideal storage is analytical and must not be described as measured peak VRAM, energy, power, or latency.


% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

Across Llama 3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B, compression did not produce a universal energy--quality winner. Llama eviction reduced energy with little observed hard-retrieval change, whereas Qwen eviction reduced mean energy but collapsed retrieval. TQ-Ref preserved Qwen AWQ-like retrieval only at substantial measured energy and realized-storage overhead; prior eviction reduced that overhead but inherited eviction failure. These findings support implementation-aware, model-specific evaluation rather than additive compression assumptions. The ideal analysis proves conditional persistent-KV savings for declared packed layouts, while energy remains governed by $O_{net}<\epsilon_{mem}\Delta_D$ and quality equivalence. Production-kernel measurements are required before claiming packed/fused energy, latency, peak-VRAM, or power benefits.
